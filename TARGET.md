# Normative Behavioral Target

## Status

This document is the normative authority for research into the adaptive work
loop. It may change only between evaluator epochs. An experiment must record the
exact content hash of the version under which it ran.

## Target behavior

The intervention package should cause an agent to improve its fit to the world
through evidence. Under representative pressure, the agent should:

1. identify a consequential uncertainty whose resolution could change a real
   decision;
2. make its expectation legible before observing the relevant outcome;
3. choose a safe, authorized contact whose possible outcomes discriminate
   between live explanations;
4. compare the observation with the prior expectation;
5. explain material discrepancies at the smallest causal level sufficient to
   predict nearby cases;
6. change the artifact, model, evaluation, representation, or workflow that the
   evidence actually bears on;
7. demonstrate the change in later predictions or decisions, including cases
   not used to form the change;
8. seek a different consequential pressure after familiar checks cease to be
   informative; and
9. stop when further contact has lower expected value than delivering or moving
   on.

The target is not maximal prediction activity. It is better consequential
prediction and action at acceptable cost.

## Observable distinctions

Evaluation must keep these constructs separate:

- **Elicitation:** Was an expectation made observable before the outcome?
- **Contact quality:** Could the selected contact distinguish important live
  explanations?
- **Prediction quality:** Did the model assign appropriate probability or
  magnitude to externally determined outcomes?
- **Resolution quality:** Did the explanation predict the original and adjacent
  observations?
- **Transfer:** Did evidence alter a later prediction or decision where it
  should, without causing negative transfer where it should not?
- **Task fitness:** Did the underlying artifact or decision improve under
  representative pressure?
- **Restraint:** Did the agent avoid unnecessary ceremony, probing, and mutation
  on trivial, complete, low-value, or unauthorized work?

No one construct substitutes for another.

## Required evaluation slices

A promotion claim must remain valid on:

- deterministic and probabilistic controlled worlds;
- boundaries and distribution shifts;
- plausible competing causal explanations;
- surprising success as well as failure;
- lesson transfer with distractors and context loss;
- superficially similar cases where transfer would be wrong;
- evaluator divergence and visible-proxy gaming;
- repeated familiar success with an untested consequential regime;
- restraint and completion controls;
- seeded software investigations; and
- at least one ecological task family not used to design the intervention.

## Primary outcomes

Primary evidence is external to the subject agent:

1. held-out prediction loss at fixed checkpoints the subject did not select;
2. transfer performance on later and fresh-context decisions;
3. task fitness on hidden tests or independently anchored outcomes; and
4. the worst critical-slice result.

Observed error encountered during self-selected contact is diagnostic. It is not
a primary outcome because a strong error-seeking agent may expose more failures,
while an evasive agent may expose few.

## Machinery and process artifacts

Instructions, skills, reminders, ledgers, receipts, schemas, tool gates, and
retrieval mechanisms are permitted interventions. They may make desired action
more likely and make evidence observable.

Completing any such artifact earns no outcome credit by itself. Process traces
may explain a result, but promotion depends on primary outcomes and critical
guardrails.

## Valid completion claim

The research program may claim a verified adaptive-loop package only for a
declared scope of models, tools, tasks, context regimes, and budgets. The exact
package content, evaluator epoch, environment, and evidence allocation must be
frozen and reproducible.

Within that scope, the package must:

- improve held-out prediction and transfer over no-loop and incumbent controls;
- preserve or improve underlying task fitness;
- survive evaluator attacks and critical slices;
- avoid unacceptable restraint cost;
- demonstrate that its claimed mechanism changed behavior; and
- survive component removal tests and sealed confirmation.

The claim is a scoped empirical result, not proof of a universal instruction
set.
