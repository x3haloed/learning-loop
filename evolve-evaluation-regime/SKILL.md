# Evolve Evaluation Regime

An evaluator is a model of fitness, not fitness itself. It earns authority by predicting what survives representative contact with reality. Evaluators may evolve, but changing one because its verdict is inconvenient destroys the evidence needed to improve it.

## Establish the divergence

Identify concrete cases where evaluator output and reality-grounded outcomes disagree. State what the evaluator was intended to proxy, the decisions it currently controls, the affected regime, and whether the disagreement is systematic enough to matter.

Separate evaluator failure from noisy observations, an unrepresentative contact surface, implementation defects, distribution shift, or disagreement about the underlying objective. Preserve raw inputs, outputs, outcomes, and evaluator versions.

## Preserve an anchor

Choose evidence that does not change merely because the evaluator changes: held-out outcomes, invariant constraints, production behavior, independent review, human judgment where the relevant variable terminates in human experience, or another faithful contact surface. No anchor is perfect; state its limits and avoid allowing the challenger to grade itself.

## Compare incumbent and challenger

Form a causal account of the incumbent's failure. Predict what a better evaluator should classify, rank, or cause the system to do differently. Make the smallest challenger capable of testing that account.

When practical, run the challenger in shadow alongside the incumbent. Compare both against held-out anchored evidence, including important subgroups, boundaries, and opportunities for gaming. Check calibration and downstream decisions, not just aggregate agreement. Account for the cost of false positives, false negatives, added complexity, and new blind spots.

Do not tune and judge on the same evidence without acknowledging the resulting uncertainty. Do not discard inconvenient ground truth, redefine success after seeing results, or promote a more flattering metric without stronger reality agreement.

## Promote and preserve

Promote the challenger only when evidence supports improved fitness prediction or better decisions at acceptable cost. Version the regime, record why it changed and where it applies, and keep raw historical evidence available for reinterpretation. Recompute derived judgments when useful; do not rewrite original observations.

After promotion, predict the downstream change and watch whether it occurs. A changed evaluator that does not improve action has not yet demonstrated value.

Stop when the chosen regime is sufficiently aligned for the stakes, known blind spots and disputed objectives are explicit, and further evaluation work has lower expected value than using it.
