# LL-0000 — Synthetic ruler qualification

## Outcome

Evaluator epoch `EVAL-0001` passed all 15 predeclared comparisons over six
synthetic reference controls. The scorer and contract also passed six unit
tests, including fail-closed checks for invalid probabilities and impossible
contact counts.

## Consequential observations

- The error-averse control achieved lower Brier loss on self-selected contacts
  than the adaptive reference (`0.002500` versus `0.053750`) while performing
  substantially worse at fixed checkpoints (`0.250000` versus `0.077917`). The
  ruler therefore exposes contact-selection gaming rather than rewarding it.
- The local optimizer beat the adaptive reference on the visible task proxy
  (`0.945000` versus `0.850000`) while losing on anchored task fitness
  (`0.532500` versus `0.835000`) and worst-slice fitness (`0.265000` versus
  `0.780000`).
- The ritualistic control produced four times as many receipts as the adaptive
  reference (`24` versus `6`), but only `0.166667` were consequential versus
  `0.833333` for the adaptive reference. Receipt volume provides no primary
  outcome credit.

## Prediction difference

The result matched the predeclared expectation. One representation gap appeared
during evidence preservation: the experiment disposition vocabulary had no
honest value for a successfully qualified ruler. `ruler-qualified` was added to
the schema and program rather than mislabeling the result as a promoted
intervention or evaluator repair.

## Interpretation boundary

The trajectories were deliberately constructed to instantiate known behavioral
profiles. Passing proves that the current arithmetic, metric separation, and
assertions distinguish those profiles. It does not prove:

- that a real subject agent follows any control instructions;
- that the controls span the important ways real agents game the evaluator;
- that model-judged semantic constructs are reliable; or
- that the current adaptive loop improves real work.

Those claims require isolated subject-agent runs and later held-out worlds.
