# Resolve Prediction Error

Prediction error is evidence that something in the artifact, causal model, constraints, environment, evaluator, contact surface, or learning machinery is inadequate. A patch is not yet a lesson. Resolve the discrepancy far enough that future prediction or action changes.

## Recover the discrepancy

State the consequential expectation, its basis, the observation, and why the difference matters. Preserve raw evidence before changing the system that produced or interpreted it. If no prediction was recorded, reconstruct only what the prior action actually implied; do not invent certainty after the fact.

Decide whether the discrepancy is material. Handle harmless noise proportionally, but leave unexplained high-consequence deviations legible.

## Discriminate explanations

Form the smallest useful set of competing explanations across the relevant layers:

- a local artifact defect;
- a missing constraint or wrong causal belief;
- an incorrect assumption about the environment;
- an unfaithful contact surface;
- an evaluator that measures the wrong thing;
- a representation or retrieval failure that prevented prior learning from affecting this action.

Choose the cheapest faithful observation or probe whose possible outcomes would distinguish between those explanations. Prefer one discriminating probe over a pile of changes that destroys causal information. Keep external effects within the user's authorization.

## Update what the evidence bears on

Explain the discrepancy with the smallest causal change that predicts both the observation and relevant nearby cases. Change the artifact when it is wrong; change the model, evaluator, representation, or workflow when that is where the evidence points. Do not privilege self-change or artifact-change in advance.

Re-run the original contact and, when stakes justify it, one adjacent probe that the revised model newly predicts. Unexpected success deserves explanation too.

## Preserve the lesson

Retain a compact causal delta:

> Expected X because Y. Observed Z. Evidence now favors Q. Therefore future predictions or actions change by R.

Preserve provenance and raw observations when later reinterpretation may matter. Remove disposable diagnostic scaffolding unless it has continuing operational value.

Stop when the material discrepancy is explained well enough to change future behavior, the revised prediction survives appropriate contact, and any remaining uncertainty is explicit and proportionate to the stakes.
