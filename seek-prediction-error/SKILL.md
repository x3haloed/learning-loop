# Seek Prediction Error

An absence of observed error is ambiguous. The model may be fit in the tested regime, contact may be narrow or forgiving, predictions may be vague, or the representation may be unable to expose what has not been learned. Expertise includes finding consequential places where a model could fail and inexpensive ways to make those failures visible.

## Find vulnerable claims

Identify the decisions that matter next and the causal claims, constraints, and assumptions supporting them. Look especially at:

- boundaries and transitions;
- unfamiliar users, environments, scales, or data;
- hidden dependencies and interactions between constraints;
- adversarial or failure conditions appropriate to the stakes;
- success that depends on an untested assumption;
- claims supported only by repeated contact of one kind;
- places where being wrong would materially change the plan.

Do not probe merely for novelty. Rank candidates by consequence, uncertainty, ability to discriminate between explanations, fidelity to the real environment, and cost or risk of contact.

## Design a consequential probe

Choose the cheapest faithful probe for the highest-value uncertainty. Before contact, state:

- what the current model predicts;
- what materially different outcomes would mean;
- which decision or model component each outcome would change;
- what must remain unchanged for the probe to be interpretable.

Do not make predictions vague, trivial, or unfalsifiable to avoid surprise. Do not perform unauthorized adversarial, production, human-subject, or externally mutating tests; use a safe representative surface or obtain the needed authority.

## Learn from contact

Observe unexpected success as well as failure. If reality differs materially, resolve the prediction error rather than immediately patching the symptom. If the prediction survives, update confidence only within the regime actually tested and choose whether a meaningfully different pressure remains valuable.

If successive well-chosen probes yield no useful error, test the search process itself: Are contacts diverse and realistic? Are predictions discriminating? Does evidence alter later behavior? Does the evaluator still track fitness? Can the representation express assumptions and contradictions?

Stop when the artifact or behavior has survived representative pressures that matter, remaining uncertainty is acceptable for the stakes, and further probing has lower expected value than acting, delivering, or moving on. Record material untested regimes rather than silently treating them as proven.
